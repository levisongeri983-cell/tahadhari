TAHADHARI — PROTOTYPE

Flood coordination prototype

A prototype coordination layer for flood response: it turns a river-risk reading into a prioritized, explainable action addressed to the responsible agency. This page is Module 1 (flood, Tana River County, Kenya) running on a simulated scenario. It is a proof of concept, not a deployed system.

Decision basis — what happens when a forecast underestimates (simulated)

In November 2023 the GloFAS forecast stayed below the Kenya Red Cross trigger while river gauges showed the river at alarm level, and the Red Cross activated on the gauge readings (Red Cross early action protocol annual report, May 2024). This switch reproduces that situation with invented numbers: the simulated forecast reads 20% below the simulated gauge. "Gauge overrides forecast" takes the higher of the two risk levels. "Forecast only" uses the forecast alone.

Alert formats — drafts shaped by resident and responder input (simulated; not sent)

Informal accounts from flood survivors said warnings reached them through the river itself, neighbours, relatives and radio that gave only broad statements, and that they moved when elders, community health promoters or Red Cross volunteers came to the door. Contacts who saw earlier drafts said they would not evacuate on a text alone: they would check the river or local gauge themselves, they know water from Wenje takes roughly 24 to 36 hours to reach the delta, they trust a message that comes from the Red Cross, and they would most like to hear it read live on radio by a presenter they know by name. These drafts now carry the gauge reading, an upstream note and a sender label for that reason. The timing is a simple trend estimate in whole days, and the 24 to 36 hour travel time is resident knowledge, not verified here. Kiswahili text is a machine-written draft and must be reviewed by a native speaker and a local radio producer before any use. Nothing here has been tested for whether people would act on it.

Relief staging window — lead time for trucking supplies (simulated)

A disaster-management officer said relief teams ideally need at least three days to truck supplies such as family shelter kits, and that flash-flooded seasonal rivers cut roads and trap trucks. Rule used here: recommend staging when the estimated time to the danger level is three days or less. This simulation covers a main river only. It does not cover flash floods on small seasonal rivers, which the officer named as the biggest gap, and it has no road or crossing risk data yet.

Trace — explainable decision layer

Ask about current conditions in plain language. Unlike a black-box score, every answer below is accompanied by its reasoning trace — the data reading, threshold check, a heuristic confidence value, and the agency routing behind it. This is a rule-based query layer over the simulated scenario data above, not an LLM call. It demonstrates the explainability pattern; a later version could add an LLM over the same schema and would still show its trace.

This page simulates the Python prototype's logic on a scripted scenario: threshold-based risk scoring on river-level trends, ranking by the hazard-agnostic Watershed Kernel, routing to responder organizations, and explanation through Trace. Today there is one working flood module and one minimal wildfire stub that plugs into the kernel with no kernel changes. A Python reference implementation of the same logic (github.com/levisongeri983-cell/tahadhari) has a GloFAS data adapter and 70 automated tests; neither is part of this web page.
